Don't Let Your Living Abroad Dream Die

Hey {$name},

I need to tell you something important: Most people who dream of living abroad never do it.

They let the dream fade. They tell themselves "maybe later" or "when the timing is right" or "it's too risky."

I know because I almost did the same thing.

At 42, I was a senior technology director in Germany. Good salary, respect, security—everything that looked like success. But inside? Emptiness. Sleepless nights. A heaviness I couldn't shake. And a voice that wouldn't stop: "You're living someone else's life."

The pandemic made it worse. More Zoom meetings. Travel restrictions. More routine. The walls closing in.

I had a choice: ignore it and keep the "good life" (many do), or listen to that voice and make the terrifying decision.

Was it reckless? Maybe. Was it necessary? Absolutely.

I left my corporate job. Moved my entire family to Mexico. Started over in my mid-40s. Built a life aligned with my values—location independent, economically sovereign, and authentically mine.

Today, I wake up to work I love, in a place I choose to be, living life on my own terms.

Here's what I learned: The biggest obstacle to living abroad isn't money, language, or logistics. It's the unresolved parts of yourself that you haven't faced yet—your shadow, your fears, the false identities you've built to fit in.

That's why I share my story—not to brag, but to show you it's possible. And more importantly, to help you do the inner work that makes the outer move not just possible, but inevitable.
